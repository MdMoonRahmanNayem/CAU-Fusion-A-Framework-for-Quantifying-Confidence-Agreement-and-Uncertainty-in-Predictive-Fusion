# CAU-Fusion

### A Framework for Quantifying Confidence, Agreement, and Uncertainty in Predictive Fusion

CAU-Fusion is a reliability-aware multimodal predictive fusion framework developed for diabetes prediction using two complementary sources of medical information: structured clinical data and retinal fundus images.

The framework combines a CatBoost-based clinical prediction model and a DenseNet121-based retinal image prediction model. Instead of simply averaging the prediction probabilities, CAU-Fusion considers prediction confidence, inter-modal agreement, disagreement, and entropy-based uncertainty to produce a more reliability-aware final prediction.

---

## Overview

Traditional multimodal prediction methods often combine model outputs using simple averaging or fixed-weight fusion. Such approaches may not adequately consider whether the individual predictions are reliable or whether the different modalities provide conflicting evidence.

CAU-Fusion addresses this limitation by introducing a reliability-aware fusion mechanism.

The framework follows two parallel prediction pipelines:

- **Clinical Data → CatBoost → Clinical Probability (Pc)**
- **Retinal Fundus Image → DenseNet121 → Image Probability (Pi)**

The generated probabilities are then processed by the CAU-Fusion mechanism to calculate confidence, agreement, disagreement, and uncertainty before generating the final prediction.

## Models Used

### CatBoost

CatBoost is used as the clinical data classifier. It processes structured clinical features and generates the clinical prediction probability.

The clinical pipeline includes data preprocessing, class balancing using SMOTETomek, stratified train-test splitting, model training, and performance evaluation.

### DenseNet121

DenseNet121 is used for retinal fundus image classification.

The retinal images are resized to **224 × 224 pixels** and normalized before training. Data augmentation techniques including rotation, zoom, and horizontal flipping are applied to improve model generalization.

---

## CAU-Fusion Mechanism

CAU-Fusion receives the prediction probabilities generated by CatBoost and DenseNet121.

The fusion process considers the following components:

### 1. Probability Calibration

The prediction probabilities are calibrated using a log-odds transformation before fusion to improve the consistency of probability-based calculations.

### 2. Confidence

The confidence component measures the strength of the individual model predictions. More reliable predictions can receive greater influence during fusion.

### 3. Agreement

Agreement evaluates how consistently the clinical and image-based models support the same prediction.

### 4. Disagreement

Disagreement identifies conflicting evidence between the two prediction modalities. Higher disagreement reduces the influence of the combined prediction.

### 5. Entropy-Based Uncertainty

Entropy is used to quantify prediction ambiguity. Higher uncertainty indicates less decisive model output and is treated as a penalty during fusion.

### 6. Final Fusion

The components are combined through the proposed parameterized fusion function:

```text
Pfinal = (S × (1 + γ × cf)) / (1 + α × D + β × U + ε)
```

Where:

* `S` = Simple Fusion score
* `cf` = Confidence score
* `D` = Disagreement
* `U` = Entropy-based uncertainty
* `α` = Disagreement penalty parameter
* `β` = Uncertainty penalty parameter
* `γ` = Confidence boosting parameter
* `ε` = Small numerical stability constant

The parameters and final decision threshold are optimized to obtain the best balanced classification performance.

## Why CAU-Fusion?

The key motivation behind CAU-Fusion is that prediction probabilities from different modalities should not always be treated as equally reliable.

For example, two models may produce highly different probabilities for the same case. A simple averaging method may hide this disagreement. CAU-Fusion explicitly analyzes:

```text
Confidence
    +
Agreement
    +
Disagreement
    +
Uncertainty
    ↓
Reliability-Aware Fusion
    ↓
Final Prediction
```

This makes the fusion process more aware of the reliability and consistency of the available evidence.

## Real-World Relevance

CAU-Fusion is designed as an AI-assisted decision-support approach for healthcare applications.

By combining structured clinical information with retinal image evidence and explicitly considering prediction reliability, the framework can potentially support more informed preliminary screening and risk assessment.

The system is intended to **assist healthcare professionals rather than replace professional medical diagnosis or clinical judgment**.

## Limitations

The current framework has several limitations:

* Both clinical and retinal image modalities are required for the current implementation.
* Missing or incomplete modalities may affect prediction performance.
* Evaluation was performed on the available dataset and experimental environment.
* External clinical validation has not yet been performed.
* Generalization to larger and more diverse clinical populations requires further investigation.
